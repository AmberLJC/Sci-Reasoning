## Synthesis

Delegated Classification fuses classic principal–agent contract theory with statistical decision theory and modern empirical scaling laws to design incentive-aware ML delegation. On the economic side, Holmström’s moral hazard framework and informativeness principle justify tying compensation to observable, performance-related signals, while Grossman and Hart provide the canonical optimization setup for deriving optimal contracts under moral hazard. Innes’s result is pivotal for the paper’s core structural claim: with monotone likelihood ratios and limited liability, optimal contracts collapse to simple threshold schemes—mirroring the debt/option-like payoffs known in incentive contracting. The paper’s most distinctive theoretical bridge connects these contract structures to Neyman–Pearson’s optimal hypothesis testing: in the binary-action case, the budget-constrained, most powerful incentive scheme is isomorphic to a likelihood-ratio test with a threshold, formally linking contract optimality to classic statistical decision rules. This equivalence both clarifies why thresholds arise and provides a principled recipe for constructing them. Practically, the authors exploit contemporary insights on learning curves and scaling laws—empirically documented by Hestness et al. and Kaplan et al.—to estimate model performance from small-scale trials and forecast outcomes at deployment scale. This enables data-efficient contract design: using limited pilot data to set thresholds and payment levels that align incentives while respecting budgets. Together, these strands yield a clean, theoretically grounded, and empirically actionable framework for delegating ML tasks to rational agents.
