## Synthesis

The paper’s key contribution—conformal meta-learners that provide distribution-free predictive intervals for individual treatment effects—emerges by fusing two mature literatures: conformal prediction and meta-learning for heterogeneous treatment effects. On the conformal side, Vovk–Gammerman–Shafer established the basic conformity-score machinery and marginal coverage guarantees, while Lei et al. provided the split conformal recipe that makes model-agnostic, finite-sample predictive inference practical. On the causal side, meta-learner templates by Künzel et al. (S/T/X-learners) codified how to assemble nuisance models into CATE estimators via two-stage regression. The present paper bridges these streams by applying split conformal prediction to the pseudo-outcomes generated by meta-learners and introducing a stochastic ordering criterion: marginal validity follows when pseudo-outcome conformity scores dominate the unattainable oracle ITE scores. It then verifies this condition for widely used two-stage learners (e.g., R/DR/X), thereby transforming point-estimation meta-learners into conformal meta-learners with rigorous predictive intervals for ITEs.
