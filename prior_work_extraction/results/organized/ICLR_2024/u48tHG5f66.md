## Synthesis: How Prior Work Led to This Paper

Latent Diffusion established a U-Net-based text-to-image generator trained at 512×512 with fixed 3×3 convolutions, a design whose receptive field defines how much global structure the model can perceive at inference. Prior convolution theory clarified why naïvely running such models at 1024×1024 fails: the effective receptive field is smaller and Gaussian-shaped, so context grows sublinearly with image size, leading to object repetition and structural distortions. Atrous (dilated) convolutions showed how to enlarge receptive fields without extra parameters or pooling, while Hybrid Dilated Convolution demonstrated that large dilations can create coverage holes and introduced mixed-rate schemes to avoid gridding artifacts. DeepLabv3’s ASPP further leveraged multiple dilation rates to capture both local detail and global context, providing practical recipes for balancing multi-scale perception. On the diffusion side, MultiDiffusion achieved high-resolution and arbitrary aspect ratios by synchronizing overlapping tiles, yet its tiling mechanics still suffer repetition and incoherence, and cascaded diffusion pipelines solve resolution by training separate super-resolution models, increasing complexity and cost. Together, these works exposed a clear opportunity: expand the pre-trained U-Net’s effective receptive field at inference—without retraining—while maintaining uniform coverage. ScaleCrafter synthesizes these ideas by dynamically re-dilating Stable Diffusion’s convolutions according to target resolution and introducing dispersed convolution to avoid gridding, delivering tuning-free, any-aspect-ratio high-resolution generation with improved structural coherence.
